# Open questions

## Answered

### Seasonal pause is unpaid months, no makeup rate

A paused summer is months the restaurant does not pay. Mandi is still paid as if those months were billed. James eats the gap. There is no higher rate when the room comes back.

Why he is fine with it: about eight months in, tool costs (Resend, Vercel, ChatGPT, Cursor, and the rest) are already about $100 a month. He is treating year one as development. Those expenses, including money he eats on free months and approved pauses, are costs he expects to write off. After year one he expects other expenses to write off, so eating a gap does not stop being acceptable just because the first year ended.

### Mandi’s pay does not flex with the month

Restaurants are year-round. Seasonality is a slow month, not a reason to pay her a different rate that year. Say it when she is hired: some months will be better than others. The commission rules stay the same. A customer pause is James eating invoices, not a cut to her check.

### Holiday reminders, not a fake neighbor stat

The operator product is multi-tenant. Users with owner permission on a restaurant get reminders for the holidays that matter in market. First market is Baltimore. Denver only if a room there wants in. Do not engineer a global holiday calendar.

There is no “your neighbor’s revenue went up” claim. James is not a marketer. If the owner ignores the reminder, that is on them.

### One shared lead book, assigned to Mandi

Every lead is visible to every admin login. New leads are assigned to Mandi. James is engineer/owner. Mandi is sales/owner. More salespeople are possible later. More engineers are not the plan.

### A pause only pauses payments

A pause changes the payment calendar and nothing else. No special pages for a type of customer. The product keeps running the way it already runs.

Schedules are custom, and James approves each one. The pitch is: we know you make most of your money in these months, or even on one weekend, and we will support the business the rest of the year without a payment in the dead stretch. A food truck that makes its year on a festival weekend is the example. It is not its own product.

The website is $99/month. Year one on a monthly plan is 10 payments, $990. Paying the year up front is 9 times the monthly price, $891, so the one payment is always cheaper. Every other product uses that same rule and is priced above $99. After hosting and onboarding the website should take almost no work from James. The restaurant changes its own content and can request other spots or features. If they ignore a holiday reminder, that is their business.
